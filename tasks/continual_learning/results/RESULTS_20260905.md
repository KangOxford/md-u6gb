| NFLX | 0.431±0.024 | 0.384±0.025 | 0.89 |

**The floor.** A synthetic score built to be *entirely* dispersion — rollouts centred on
the truth, differing only in a per-context width, so nothing about being wrong is present —
keeps **0.48** under the identical procedure at 10 seeds. The honest answer for that score
is 0; it is not 0 because the nuisance is estimated from held-out seeds and a noisy proxy
under-removes. The floor falls as the proxy improves: **0.48 at 10 seeds, 0.37 at 12**, so
it must be measured at the seed count in use and never carried over from another.

So the reading is **"0.87 against a 0.48 floor"**. The conclusion (a real conditional signal
survives) stands; the margin is roughly half what "0.87 of it survives" implies. This is the
same error shape as reading a stratified null of 0.10 as zero.

## A. Retraction — `nbconvert` does not return 0 on a failing notebook

Addendum 2 §E said `jupyter nbconvert --execute` "returned 0 on a run whose first code cell

`tail`'s. Measured with the producer's return code captured directly, on a notebook whose

```
$ jupyter nbconvert --execute --inplace --ExecutePreprocessor.timeout=60 fail_probe.ipynb \

$ rc=$?                                  # captured immediately, no pipe in between
  nbconvert returncode: 1
  stderr mentions the planted error: 3 lines
  cells with output after the run: 0 / 2
```

**`nbconvert` reports the failure correctly.** ~~The tool reports success on a failed
execution.~~ What is real, and is the part worth keeping: `--inplace` leaves a notebook with
**zero outputs** on disk after a failed run, so an artifact that looks published can carry
nothing, and only counting the outputs detects that. The detection habit was right; the
attribution was not.

**This is the third time this session I have read `$?` after a pipe** — once on
`fidelity.py`, once here, and once while criticising the first. The mechanical fix: never
put a pipe between a command and its status. Redirect to a file, capture `$?` on the very
next line, then read the file.

# Addendum 5 — the `num_errors` join, recovered; X4 and the dilution assumption, specified

## A. The mapping, established by measurement

The log carries 528 slots for 500 contexts. `gen_driver.py:1688` asserts
`n_samples % batch_size == 0`, and `:1698` chunks a supplied index list as
`flat[i:i+batch_size]`. With 500 contexts and `batch_size = 48` the last chunk holds 20, so
the batch is padded — and the pad is **not** at the end of the logged array.

Verified by recomputing the definition from the generated books rather than by reading the
batching code: `num_errors = (l2_book_states[1:] == l2_book_states[:-1]).all(axis=1).sum()`
recomputed from `data_gen/*_orderbook_real_id_<cid>_gen_id_0.csv`.

slots 500..527  ==  recomputed for rank_indices[0..27]        matched, 3/3 members, 3 tickers
slots   0..27   ==  neither

## D. The dilution assumption — what would establish it

`PLAN` §0.3's `k = 3` budget rests on `plan_drafts/02` §3.2's claim that in a *training*
pool a false positive **dilutes** the treatment rather than **contaminating** it, because a
wrongly selected context is an ordinary market window and that is what replay supplies
anyway. `02` says explicitly that this is untested. **§0.3 must not be quoted as a
conclusion until it is**, and it is not quoted as one here.

The claim is testable on the existing archive, on CPU, without generating anything:

1. Build a reference pool at high `k` (the top decile by the stratified score at `k = 5`,
   the largest available), and a **matched-size** pool of contexts drawn at random.
2. Construct mixtures: a pool of fixed size `N₀` containing a fraction `p` of reference
   members and `1 − p` random ones, for `p ∈ {1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.0}`.
3. For each mixture compute the **selection contrast** — mean score inside the pool minus
   the population mean, on rollouts held out of the selection.

**Dilution predicts the contrast falls linearly in `p` and reaches the population mean at
`p = 0`.** Contamination predicts a *faster than linear* fall, or a contrast that goes
negative before `p = 0`, i.e. the impurities are actively worse than a random draw.

That is a proxy: it tests the contrast, not the downstream training effect, and a linear
contrast does not prove a linear *training* response. It is the strongest statement
obtainable without a training run, and it must be labelled as such wherever it is used.
**If the fall is faster than linear, `k = 3` is not defensible.** ~~and the budget returns to
the k implied by the largest-k estimator (17–41, median 22)~~ — **that sentence used the
rejected extrapolation as a budget, 327 lines after this same file said it is not used as
one** (R2-F14). The extrapolation is rejected by its own residuals, so it cannot set a budget
in either direction. What follows from a faster-than-linear fall is that **the required `k` is
not established**, and the archive cannot establish it either: with 10 seeds the split-half
curve stops at `k = 5`, and a direct reading at `k ≈ 20` needs rollouts that do not exist.
That is a measurement the plan does not currently have a way to make.

+0.0049) and missed by 0.0018 in the primary. **The decision rule as written did not
anticipate a difference two orders of magnitude below the between-ticker spread**, and
reading a 0.0018 gap as a failure or rounding it to a pass would both be over-reading.

**Adopted, on the balance ground alone**: `R_v2_within` for any future pool, because it
matches stratum composition by construction and the score contrast is indistinguishable
between the two. **Not claimed**: that it selects a better pool for training. Nothing here
was trained, and the contrast is a proxy.

## The old rule is not better, and its apparent advantage is its own yardstick

`R_v1_global` shows the largest `Y_v1` (0.857) — but `Y_v1` *is* the v1 score, so that
comparison is rigged in its favour, which is why the pre-registration required both. On the
neutral yardstick the gap is **+0.0090 (primary) and +0.0158 (sensitivity) against a spread
of 0.07–0.08**, i.e. indistinguishable, while its `bal` is worse by **+0.096 and +0.046**.
So the collapsed binning buys no measurable extra score concentration and does carry more of
the realised move — the trade D1 §2 predicted, with the score half of it turning out to be
nothing rather than a cost worth paying.

## What is still missing

The contrast is measured on held-out **rollouts**, not on held-out **contexts**, so it says a
rule's pool keeps its score advantage when re-scored — not that the pool generalises to
contexts the selection never saw. A context-held-out split is the next cheap check and has
not been run. And no rule has been evaluated by training, which is the only thing that can
answer whether any of this improves a model.

# Addendum 8 — correcting addendum 7's inference: non-significance is not equivalence

## The pre-registered rule was invalid as written, and no result rescues it

Read back verbatim from `4611383a`:

> `The transfer cost is T_oracle − T_transfer.` **A cost indistinguishable from zero means
> the edges transfer**; a positive cost means they do not, and its size is the answer.
>
> — transfer cost within the between-ticker spread ⇒ **edges transfer**; `stratify_v2` may
> be fitted once and reused.

That reads a failure to detect a difference as a demonstration that there is none. It is the
wrong test for the question: showing two things are close enough to substitute requires a
tolerance fixed in advance and an interval that fits inside it, not a wide interval that
happens to straddle zero. **The rule is withdrawn. Addendum 7's "the edges may be fitted once
and reused" and its "SUPPORTED" label are withdrawn with it.**

## What is being generalised to has to be stated, because the interval depends on it

The pre-registration did not say. Two targets, and only the second supports "fit once, reuse":

- **(a) these 8 tickers, these contexts.** The ticker set is fixed; the uncertainty is finite
  contexts and finite rollouts. Says nothing about a ticker not measured.
- **(b) a new ticker or a new context set.** The ticker is the resampling unit, df = 7. This
  is the target implied by "fitted once and reused", so it is the one reported.

## The interval, target (b), in SD units of the evaluation score

| analysis | per-ticker costs | mean | 95% CI | as a fraction of the contrast |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| primary (exclude 28) | +0.055, +0.001, **−0.360**, −0.005, −0.050, +0.107, −0.037, +0.095 | −0.0242 | **[−0.1476, +0.0991]** | **[−18.0%, +12.1%]** |
| sensitivity (include 28) | −0.061, −0.032, +0.123, +0.044, −0.151, +0.020, −0.012, +0.043 | −0.0033 | **[−0.0718, +0.0653]** | **[−9.0%, +8.2%]** |

The primary interval spans nearly a fifth of the effect it is measuring, and its width is
driven by GOOG at −0.360 — one ticker of eight.

## Verdict, corrected

**The measured transfer difference is small, and reusability is NOT established.** Both
statements are needed; the first alone was what addendum 7 published.

**Tolerance sensitivity, offered as a description of the interval and nothing more.** A
tolerance δ of **0.148 SD units (18% of the contrast)** or larger would contain the primary
interval; **0.072 SD units (9%)** or larger would contain the sensitivity interval. Any δ
below those cannot be certified by this data. **No tolerance was set in advance**, so none of
this is pre-registered support, and picking a δ now because it happens to fit would be
exactly the manoeuvre the pre-registration exists to prevent. A defensible δ has to come from
what a pool can absorb without changing a training outcome, which nothing here measures.

## What survives addendum 7 unchanged

**The realised-move confound, and it is stronger than the 6/8 count I reported.** Testing the
comparison that is actually claimed — the literal issue-#73 rule against the stratified one,
on `|bal − 1|`, paired by ticker with df = 7:

| analysis | mean | 95% CI | |
|---|---:|---|---|
| primary | **+1.661** | **[+1.171, +2.151]** | excludes 0, 8/8 tickers |
| sensitivity | **+1.725** | **[+1.267, +2.184]** | excludes 0, 8/8 tickers |

That is a difference, not a failure to find one, and the interval excludes zero by a wide
margin under both analyses. (Addendum 6's 6/8 count was a different and much smaller
comparison — within-stratum against global-stratified selection — and it remains
not established.)

**The matched-`k` `num_errors` advantage** stands: 8/8 tickers at k=1 (0.229–0.419 vs
0.128–0.267) and 8/8 at k=5 (0.603–0.784 vs 0.353–0.546).

**The floor inflation is scoped, not general.** The measured 1.26–4.90× applies **only to a
contrast whose two sides genuinely cannot share draws** — a treated arm against a control arm
generated separately. It does **not** condemn matched pairing: when two sides should share a
nuisance, sharing it is the point of the design, and the matched floor is the correct one.
Using the crossed figure for a legitimately matched contrast would overstate the noise as
badly as using the matched figure for an unmatched one understates it.
