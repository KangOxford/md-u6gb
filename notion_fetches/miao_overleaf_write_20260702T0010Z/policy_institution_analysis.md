Core writing logic:

- China's elderly-care policy is now a combination of pension insurance, elderly-care services, long-term care insurance, healthy aging, and silver economy policy.
- The paper's result means pension policy should be evaluated not only as income support, but also as a stabilizer of expectations, psychological well-being, and life satisfaction.
- Because the living-alone interaction is stronger, policy recommendations should prioritize living-alone older adults.
- Since child transfer is not significant, family support should not be assumed to substitute for institutional pension and basic elderly-care services.

## Recommendations Added

1. Improve basic pension adequacy and stability, especially for low-benefit, older, and living-alone groups.
2. Include psychological health and life satisfaction in elderly-care policy evaluation.
3. Build targeted identification and active service mechanisms for living-alone older adults.
4. Do not treat child transfers as a stable substitute for institutional pension support.
5. Coordinate long-term care insurance with pension income and community-based elderly-care services.
6. Treat personal pension as a long-run supplement, not as the main current policy lever for already-retired older adults.
7. Develop the silver economy under affordability, quality supervision, and inclusive-service constraints.
